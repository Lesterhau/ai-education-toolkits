## What's in them

Practical tools for turning AI concerns into policy,
curriculum decisions, and a 30-day starting plan:

- A named AI Curriculum Integration Lead, with clear
  ownership and next steps
- A three-zone AI use policy: AI-Free, AI-Transparent,
  and AI-Collaborative, with model language leaders can
  adapt
- An AI-Free Declaration Track for students with
  principled ethical objections
- Student privacy and vendor requirements, including
  written FERPA confirmation
- Academic-integrity guidance informed by research on
  AI-detector false positives
- Curriculum gap mapping, capstone design, faculty
  development, and succession planning
- Funding considerations for universities and
  Texas-specific political risk analysis for high schools

## The methods and research behind them

The toolkit designs were stress-tested across six
stakeholder roles and five revision rounds. They draw on
UNESCO's Guidance for Generative AI in Education, CSTA
standards, AI4K12 grade bands, and TeachAI policy
guidance.

The research base includes:

- Imagining the Digital Future's 2025-2026 research,
  including the Human Resilience Report and Being Human
  in 2035
- WEF's Future of Jobs Report 2025, McKinsey Global
  Institute, PwC's Global AI Jobs Barometer 2025,
  Brookings, and BLS occupational outlook data
- Gallup/Walton/GSV's Voices of Gen Z research
- Stanford's AI-detector research and Digital Economy
  Lab's Canaries in the Coal Mine, alongside studies by
  Liang et al., Scarfe et al., Lee et al., and Wharton's
  Shaw and Nave
- EDUCAUSE, MIT Open Learning and OpenCourseWare, OpenAI
  Academy, and the MIT AI Risk Initiative in the
  university edition

The PDFs contain the detailed citations and show how the
research connects to the recommendations.
